Devpost Learn · Scope

huf

Say it once. Huf remembers.

Approved

The unique kernel

Huf remembers context, not just commands. One natural sentence becomes a memory and a commitment, both tied to the right person — and one question later brings both back, with the saved items as proof.

“Most tools expect us to know whether we’re creating a task, writing a note, or setting a reminder. But that’s not how our brains work.”

The core loop

One big mic, natural speech, no categories up front. Every capture makes recall more valuable.

Who it’s for · why it matters

The person

A daily commuter with “a bazzillion things” on their mind, who forgets promises — like calling their aunt — and feels guilty. Siri needs command phrasing; Alexa isn’t on the go.

The feeling

“I said it once, and I can trust that I won’t lose it.”

What “working” looks like

Tap the mic, speak the Sarah sentence. See a memory card and a reminder card, both linked to Sarah. Fix anything, save. Refresh. Ask what Sarah told you and what you owe her — Huf answers from what you actually saved and shows the cards beneath. Then: “Amazon by Friday, dentist tomorrow morning, rent on the first of every month” → three reminders with the right dates.

“It shouldn’t be a hardcoded response that only works for our demo.”

The boundary

Now The proof of concept
  • Tap-to-talk with real transcription; typed fallback.
  • One input → many items, each a memory or reminder, linked to a person, with dates resolved from today (recurrence labeled).
  • Review before saving: edit, delete, confirm. Nothing saved silently.
  • Saved items persist across refresh.
  • Ask Huf: answer grounded only in saved items, sources shown; honest “I don’t have that.”
  • Both demo beats — Sarah first in priority, brain dump second.
Later Worth doing, not now
  • Clarifying questions when something is ambiguous.
  • Browsing and managing full history.
  • Broad questions (“What am I forgetting this week?”) — may partly work, not tuned for.
  • Actual notifications at the due time.
  • Discipline: follow-through, patterns, gentle nudges — “not a habit tracker or productivity coach right now.”
Cut Named, with reasons
  • Contacts integration — people come from what you say.
  • Calendar syncing — auth and complexity that don’t prove context memory.
  • Recurring notifications — recurrence is labeled, not scheduled.
  • “Hey Huf” wake word / always-on listening — tap-to-talk is enough; avoids privacy concerns.
  • Accounts & multi-device sync — one person, one device proves the loop.
